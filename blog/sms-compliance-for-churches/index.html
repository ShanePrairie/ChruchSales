Church Text Messaging: Consent, STOP, and Careful Language

Church texts should be invited, identifiable, easy to stop, and written like a careful note from a real person.

November 25, 2026By Modernized Ministry4 min read

Church texts should be invited, identifiable, easy to stop, and written like a careful note from a real person.

Get a yes for texts, not a leftover phone number

A phone number on a guest card, a membership form, or a directory is not automatically permission to text. Ask for a clear yes that names what the texts are: visit reminders, a small group, or urgent closures. Let people agree to email and refuse texts. Record the yes, including when it happened, so the team is not guessing later.

This page is a practical care guide, not legal advice. Rules depend on where you are and how the message is sent, and your provider and counsel should confirm the details for your situation. The habits below are the ones that keep messages human. The welcome system guide shows where email is the better default.

  • Ask for text permission in its own sentence.
  • Say what kinds of texts you will send.
  • Say how often people should expect them.
  • Identify the church in the message.
  • Tell people they can reply STOP to opt out.

Honor STOP and other opt-outs immediately

If someone replies STOP, or says in person that they do not want texts, stop. Do not send one more note to confirm, argue, or ask them to reconsider. Make sure volunteers with personal phones are not texting around the system you just promised to respect. One ignored opt-out undoes a year of careful wording.

Also provide a non-text way to opt out, such as an email to the office, for people who do not want to reply to the thread. Process that request the same day. The first-week plan should never keep texting a guest who has gone quiet or said no.

Write like a person with a specific reason

A useful text says who it is from, what is happening, and what the recipient might do. Sunday at 10 is on as planned. Kids check-in is at the side door. Reply STOP to opt out. That is enough. Do not send vague spiritual pressure, fake urgency, or messages that shame people for missing a week. Do not include sensitive pastoral details in a text that can be read on a lock screen.

Avoid sending late at night or early in the morning unless the message is truly urgent, such as a same-day closure. Batch ordinary reminders at a humane hour. If you would not knock on the door at that time, do not buzz their pocket.

Keep urgent texts for urgent facts

Weather closures, a moved room, or a same-day change are the best uses of a text, and only for people who opted into that kind of message. The cancellation guide shows how to put the same fact on the website so you are not depending on a text everyone may miss. Do not use the urgent channel for general promotion just because it gets tapped.

Do not buy a list. Do not upload numbers scraped from a spreadsheet of unknown origin. If a volunteer offers a personal contact list, decline it unless each person agreed to hear from the church by text. Convenience is not consent.

Confirm the setup before you announce it

Before you print text us on a slide, send a test to staff phones, confirm STOP works, and confirm the church name appears. Decide who is allowed to send, and what they are not allowed to say. A shared login with no owner becomes a problem the first time someone sends the wrong group.

Keep a simple log of the kinds of messages you send, not a surveillance file on each member. The log should be enough to answer, months later, whether a person agreed to visit reminders or only to weather alerts. When a new volunteer takes over the tool, walk them through that distinction before they import anything. Imports are where accidental lists begin.

If you want help drawing the line between email, text, and the website, describe the messages you hope to send and who has already agreed to receive them. Start smaller than the tool allows. A short, permitted note is better than a loud system people block.

Frequently asked questions

Can we text everyone who gave us a guest card?

Only the people who agreed to texts. A card that collects a phone number for a general contact is not a yes to ongoing messages.

What should we do about a volunteer texting from a personal phone?

Give them the same rules: permission, identification, and an opt-out. Personal phones are not a workaround.
