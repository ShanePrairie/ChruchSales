Church Marketing Strategy: A Practical 90-Day Outreach Plan

A clear 90-day strategy for helping more people discover your church and feel welcome.

November 23, 2026By Modernized Ministry4 min read

A clear 90-day strategy for helping more people discover your church and feel welcome.

Start with people, not platforms

A church marketing strategy is not a promise to fill seats. It is a repeatable way to help people discover your congregation, understand what you believe, and feel comfortable taking a first step. The best plan connects Sunday hospitality with what someone sees during a Tuesday-night Google search.

Choose one group to serve well first: new families, people returning to faith, students, or neighbors looking for community. Ask what questions they would have before visiting. Those questions should shape your website, search listings, invitations, and volunteer conversations.

  • Write one sentence explaining who your church welcomes and what a first visit feels like.
  • Give one person ownership of the website, one of guest hospitality, and one of follow-up.
  • Measure meaningful conversations and repeat visits, not impressions alone.

Days 1–30: Fix the first impression

Search for your church name on a phone. Can a newcomer find service times, directions, what to wear, children's ministry details, and a clear contact option in under a minute? If not, your initial marketing budget should go toward clarity rather than new advertising.

Update your Google Business Profile with accurate hours, categories, photographs, and an official website link. Put a short 'Plan Your Visit' path on the homepage. Write three helpful questions with plain answers instead of leading with insider language.

  • Audit your mobile homepage and navigation.
  • Make service times, address, parking and accessibility easy to find.
  • Ask a person unfamiliar with your church to test the guest journey.

Days 31–60: Make outreach easy to share

Build one campaign around something genuinely useful: a community meal, family event, neighborhood service project, or sermon series that answers real-life questions. Create a simple landing page with the event date, audience, cost if any, and a clear RSVP or contact step.

Support it with a small rhythm: one community post, one short pastor video, a newsletter mention to consenting subscribers, and a personal invitation toolkit for members. Social sharing works best when the post gives someone a reason to care beyond 'please attend'.

  • Create a single shareable event URL.
  • Use photos you have permission to publish.
  • Invite personal conversations, not mass messaging to unconsenting lists.

Days 61–90: Welcome, learn, improve

Set up a guest follow-up process that begins with permission. A friendly thank-you within a day, one useful next-step invitation, and an easy way to decline further messages is more respectful than an aggressive text sequence.

Review a simple monthly scorecard: search impressions and website visits, Plan Your Visit actions, first-time guests who voluntarily introduce themselves, guest responses, second visits, and new small-group connections. Numbers should prompt better care, not reduce people to a funnel.

  • Hold a 20-minute monthly review with ministry and hospitality leaders.
  • Ask guests what felt unclear or intimidating.
  • Improve one weak point at a time rather than buying five new platforms.

Make the plan sustainable

A small church can execute this with a part-time coordinator and a few committed volunteers. Consistency is more valuable than chasing every trend. Start where your team can deliver a real welcome, then widen the invitation.

Frequently asked questions

What is church marketing?

Church marketing is the way a congregation communicates its mission, helps people discover services and community programs, and makes participation easier.

How long before a church marketing plan works?

Use the first 90 days to establish accurate information, a consistent invitation rhythm, and a baseline for guest connections. Actual growth depends on many factors.
