Weather Cancellations and Urgent Church Updates

When plans change, one visible website notice should state what is canceled, what is not, and when you will update it.

November 25, 2026By Modernized Ministry4 min read

When plans change, one visible website notice should state what is canceled, what is not, and when you will update it.

Pick one place the notice will always appear

In bad weather, people check the website, a text, and a social feed, in an unpredictable order. Choose one canonical place: a short banner on the homepage that states the decision in text. Repeat it on the events page if an event is affected. Social posts can point at that banner. They should not be the only copy, because not everyone sees them and not everyone has an account. The email and text guide explains which channel fits a planned welcome versus a same-day change.

Decide the words before the storm. Canceled, delayed, or still meeting should be unambiguous. Include the date. Include what is still happening if only one gathering is off. The homepage should be able to hold a banner without covering the ordinary time so thoroughly that next week's guest is misled.

  • What is canceled or delayed, by name.
  • The date the decision applies to.
  • What is still meeting.
  • When you will post the next update.
  • A phone or email if the notice is confusing.

Name who is allowed to publish

A closure notice needs a person who can edit the site when the office is empty, plus a backup. Write down how they log in and where the banner lives. Practice once on a calm week. The morning of a closure is a bad time to discover that the only person with the password is out of town.

Say who makes the decision, even if you do not publish the org chart. Volunteers should not hear three versions. One decision, one sentence, one place. Update the events page in the same edit if a weeknight item is affected.

Write the banner so it still makes sense if someone sees it without the group chat that preceded it. Name the church, the day, and the gathering. Avoid inside jokes and abbreviations that a guest, or a grandparent driving in, will not know. If only a Bible study is canceled and Sunday is still meeting, the first words should say that Sunday is meeting. People skim the first line and stop.

If you post a photo of a closed building, repeat the decision in the caption and in the page text. A picture of a snowy door does not say whether the evening class is still on. Text carries the decision. The photo only illustrates it.

Text only the people who opted into urgent news

A text is a useful extra for people who asked for urgent messages. It is not a replacement for the website, and it is not permission you can assume from a general directory. Keep the text as short as the banner, identify the church, and include STOP if that is how your system works. The text messaging guide covers consent in more detail.

Send the text after the website is updated, or at the same time, so a person who taps through sees the same fact. Contradictory channels are how families split up in parking lots.

Take the notice down on purpose

A cancellation banner left up through the next fair-weather Sunday will convince guests you are closed. Put a reminder on the calendar to remove it, and name the person. Restore the ordinary service time in the same edit. If you promised an update by a certain hour, post that update even if the decision is we are still meeting.

After the event, note what failed: a password, a slow host, a social post that went out first with the wrong time. Fix the checklist while the memory is fresh. Do not wait for the next storm to rediscover it.

Prepare a non-weather version of the same habit

The same banner can carry a building closure, a moved room, or a funeral that changes the evening schedule. The standard is the same: one sentence, a date, a person who can post it, and a takedown. Do not invent a new system for every kind of surprise.

If the site is so hard to edit that you currently rely on a personal social profile, describe the delay. An urgent update path should be something more than one staff member can operate. The website remains the place the other channels should point.

Frequently asked questions

Should we email the cancellation too?

Yes if you can send promptly to people who agreed to email. Send the same sentence as the banner. Do not write a different decision in the email.

What if we decide late?

Post as soon as the decision exists, and say the time you posted. A late accurate note is better than silence, and better than a rumor in a group chat.
