Church Social Media Strategy: A Simple Weekly Content Plan

What to post each week without turning ministry into a full-time content factory.

November 23, 2026By Modernized Ministry4 min read

What to post each week without turning ministry into a full-time content factory.

Social media should point to people

Church social media is useful for explaining who you are and inviting real relationships. It is not a replacement for pastoral care or a community that knows people's names. The strongest posts answer a question, tell a true story with consent, or help someone take a useful next step.

Choose one or two platforms your community actually uses. Your team is better off sharing three thoughtful posts consistently than posting daily and burning out.

A sustainable weekly calendar

Monday: one accessible quote or takeaway from Sunday's sermon, paired with context. Wednesday: a behind-the-scenes service or community story. Friday: an invitation to a specific gathering with time, location, and a link to the visitor page.

Once a month, publish a short introduction to a ministry leader or volunteer, with permission. Mix formats: photo, short captioned video, event card, and a straightforward question.

Make every post welcoming

Write captions for someone who has never attended church. Define internal terms such as 'fellowship hall' or 'small group' when helpful. Add readable captions to videos, alt text to images, and details beyond 'join us.'

Instead of 'See you Sunday,' try 'Visiting for the first time? We gather at 10 a.m. Here's where to park, where kids check in, and what to expect.' Link to a page that matches the invitation.

Avoid common church marketing mistakes

Do not publish identifiable children without appropriate permission. Avoid overwhelming feeds with donation requests, misleading engagement contests, or unverified success stories. When sharing testimonials, get explicit consent and give the person control over how their story is used.

A prayer request may be sensitive. Never turn someone's private message into a public post just because it would make compelling content.

Measure the next step, not applause

Track visits to your event and first-visit pages, direct questions, conversations with volunteers, and attendance at events—not only likes or follower counts. Compare a few months of activity before deciding what is useful.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a church post?

A repeatable schedule of two or three genuinely helpful posts per week is often a better starting point than an ambitious daily calendar.

Should churches use Facebook or Instagram?

Choose based on the people you want to communicate with, your existing congregation, and your team's capacity; there is no universal winner.
