What Churches Should Measure on a Website (Without Being Creepy)

Measure whether the website helps people find the next step, not who they are or where they went afterward.

November 25, 2026By Modernized Ministry4 min read

Measure whether the website helps people find the next step, not who they are or where they went afterward.

Decide the question before you add a tool

Analytics are useful when they answer a question the team will act on. Did anyone reach the plan-your-visit page this month? Did the Christmas schedule page get used on the days you promoted it? Are most people on phones? Those questions improve a site. A dashboard nobody opens is not a ministry.

You do not need to identify individuals to answer them. Choose a tool that reports totals, and say so on your privacy page. Do not drop in extra advertising pixels because a template offered them. The search guide is about being found. This guide is about learning from the visit without following the person home.

  • Visits to the homepage, visit page, and events page.
  • The devices people use, in aggregate.
  • Whether a form was completed, not who hesitated.
  • Which links in an email were tapped, in aggregate.
  • Search phrases if you have them from a search console you control.

Look at paths, not people

A useful review asks whether the visit page is reached from the homepage, and whether the events page is a dead end. If almost nobody opens a page, either the menu hides it or the page is not needed. Fix the path or retire the page. Do not use the data to rank members or to surprise someone with a detail from their browsing.

Form totals can tell you that the visit form is unused or that it fails on phones. They should not become a prospect list beyond what the person typed and sent. The follow-up plan starts when someone gives you their name on purpose, not when they merely load a page.

Skip creepy defaults

Do not use session recordings that show what a person typed before they submitted. Do not sell or share visitor data. Do not embed tools you cannot explain to a member who asks. If a tool sets advertising cookies, it does not belong on a church site that promised a simple welcome.

Prefer aggregate, cookie-free measurement when it answers your questions. Review the tool once a year and remove tags from old campaigns. The budget guide fits here: a paid analytics suite is not the first place a small church should spend if a basic count would do.

Read the numbers in context

A spike during Easter does not mean the ordinary site is healthy. A quiet week may mean a holiday, a broken link, or a storm. Compare like with like, and write down what you changed on the site so you do not credit a new headline for a change that was actually the weather. Never publish a number as a promise of future attendance.

Ignore vanity totals that do not connect to a task. A large number of homepage loads means little if the visit page is never opened and the phone number is an image nobody can tap. Pair the count with the task you hoped the page would support.

Share the few numbers with the people who can change the site, not as a scoreboard in the lobby. A monthly note that the visit page is hard to find from the homepage is more useful than a chart of strangers. If a number would embarrass a member if it were about them personally, you are collecting the wrong thing. Aggregate totals about pages are in bounds. Profiles of people are not.

Review the same three pages every month so the habit stays small enough to keep. Write down one change you made because of what you saw, even if the change was a clearer menu label.

Tell the truth on the privacy page

Say what you collect, that you do not sell it, and how someone can ask a question. If you are not sure what a plugin collects, remove it until you are. A short honest paragraph is better than a copied policy that lists tools you do not use.

If you want help choosing measures that match a privacy-first welcome, describe the questions you wish the site could answer. Start with three numbers you will actually review each month. Leave the rest uncollected.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need to track individual guests to improve the site?

No. Page totals and a phone test will show whether the visit path works. Identity is a different decision, and it belongs in a form someone submits on purpose.

Should staff look at analytics every week?

A monthly look at a few pages is enough for most churches. Weekly checking becomes noise unless you are in a short campaign with a specific page.
