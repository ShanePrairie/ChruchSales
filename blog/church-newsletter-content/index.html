Church Email Newsletters People Will Open

A church newsletter gets opened when it has one job, a plain subject line, and a list people agreed to join.

November 25, 2026By Modernized Ministry4 min read

A church newsletter gets opened when it has one job, a plain subject line, and a list people agreed to join.

Give each email one job

A newsletter that contains the sermon recap, three events, a giving push, a volunteer crisis, and a poem will be skimmed and then ignored. Pick the one thing a reader should do or know this week. Other items can be a short list of links at the bottom, or they can wait. People can finish a short email. They archive a long one.

This guide is about the ongoing newsletter. The email and text welcome guide covers first-time guest messages, which should stay separate from the general list. Do not drop a guest into the weekly newsletter just because they visited, unless they asked for it.

  • One main story or announcement.
  • The date and time if you are asking people to attend.
  • A single button or link for the main action.
  • A short list of secondary links, if you must.
  • A clear way to unsubscribe.

Write subject lines that state the point

The subject line should tell the truth about the email. Sunday at 10, parking note is more useful than You won't believe this. Avoid fake urgency and avoid hiding the topic. If the email is about a meeting, say the meeting. People who wanted that email will open it. People who did not will not mark you as a nuisance for surprising them.

Preview text, the line some inboxes show beside the subject, should continue the point rather than repeating a logo slogan. Put the most important date near the top of the body, not after three paragraphs of greeting. Link to the events page for details so you are not maintaining two versions of the time.

Send only to people who asked

Build the list from people who opted in: a checkbox on a form, a signup on the website, or a conversation where email was requested. Do not add an entire directory, a guest book, or a purchased list. Keep the promise you made about frequency. If you said monthly, do not email three times in a crisis week without saying why.

Every send needs a working unsubscribe. Honor it. Also name the church in the from line so people recognize you on a phone. A personal name is fine when that person is real and replies. A fake personal name that nobody monitors is not fine.

Make the email readable on a phone

Most people will open church email on a phone between other tasks. Use short paragraphs, real links with words that describe the destination, and a type size they can read without pinching. Do not send the bulletin as a wide image with the only copy of the announcement baked into the picture. If they cannot select the time, they cannot save it.

Match the links to pages that are current. A beautiful email that points at last month's event page trains people to stop tapping. The social plan can repeat the same one announcement. It should not contradict the email.

Read the email aloud once before you send it, including the subject line. If you stumble, the sentence is too long. Check every link on a phone. Look at the from name and the first line a lock screen will show, and make sure that preview does not reveal a private pastoral detail. A newsletter is a public note even when the list feels small.

Give the newsletter an editor and a rhythm

Name one editor, even if several people contribute a sentence. Pick a send day and keep it unless there is a real exception, such as a weather closure. Look at what people tap, if your tool shows that in aggregate, and stop repeating sections nobody uses. You do not need to track individuals to learn that the volunteer block is ignored.

If the list is messy or the tool is harder than the letter you are trying to write, tell us how you email today. A simpler rhythm beats a complicated automation nobody trusts. The goal is a note the church would be glad to have sent.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a church newsletter be?

Short enough to read on a phone without scrolling through unrelated appeals. If you need a second topic, link it instead of writing a second article.

Can we email the whole membership roll?

Only if those people were told they would receive church email and can opt out. A directory is not the same thing as permission for marketing messages.
