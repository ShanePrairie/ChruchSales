Make a Church Website Fast on Phones

A fast phone page is a hospitality choice: guests on a weak signal should still see the service time.

November 25, 2026By Modernized Ministry4 min read

A fast phone page is a hospitality choice: guests on a weak signal should still see the service time.

Speed is part of the welcome

A guest standing in a parking lot, or sitting in a car before deciding to come in, will not wait on a heavy homepage. If the service time appears after a large video and several unused scripts, some people will give up and search for a different congregation. Speed is not a technical hobby. It is whether the facts arrive in time to be useful.

You do not need a new platform to improve a slow page. Start by watching the homepage and the plan-your-visit page load on a phone using cellular data, not the office Wi-Fi. Note what appears first and what you could have skipped. The accessibility checklist overlaps with this work, because the same files that are heavy are often the ones that move, flash, or hide text.

  • Open the homepage on a phone off Wi-Fi.
  • Time how long the service time takes to appear.
  • List every autoplay video and third-party widget.
  • Note images that are wider than the screen needs.

Shrink photographs before they are uploaded

A photo from a phone can be several thousand pixels wide. The homepage may display it at a few hundred. Upload a version sized for the layout, and use a modern compressed format your site already supports. Decorative backgrounds do not need to be sharp enough to read a face in the back row. The photography guide explains how to choose images with care; this guide is about the file that results.

Do not place a slideshow of ten full-size photos above the fold. One well-chosen picture, with the time written in real text beside it, will load sooner and communicate more. If a page has a gallery, load those images after the essential copy, or on a separate page people open on purpose.

Be careful with video and embeds

A livestream embed, a giving widget, a chat bubble, and a social feed can each pull in large scripts before your own sentence appears. Put video on the sermon or livestream page, and let the homepage link to it. If a clip must sit on the homepage, do not autoplay it with sound, and do not make the visitor wait on it to read the address.

Test embeds on the visit page separately. A map can be useful, and it can also be slow. A written address next to the map means the guest still has the fact if the map is late. Remove widgets nobody on staff can name a purpose for. The design checklist is the right place to decide what belongs on the first screen at all.

Fonts, popups, and extra code

Custom fonts are fine when they load in a restrained way. Five weights of three families for a single page is not restrained. Use the weights you actually set in the design, and let the text remain readable if a font is slow. Popups that cover the service time until a person finds a close button are a speed problem and a clarity problem. If you need an urgent notice, use a short banner in the page, not a modal that must be dismissed before reading.

Ask whoever edits the site to stop pasting entire scripts into the footer because a tool recommended it. Each addition should have an owner and a reason. Review the list twice a year and delete what you no longer use. Old chat tools and abandoned analytics tags are a common source of delay.

Give the improvement a simple test

After each change, repeat the phone test on cellular data and write down whether the service time appears sooner. Keep a note of what you removed. You do not need a public score to know the page got better, and you should not publish a number you cannot explain. If a tool offers a lab report, use it internally to find the largest file, then fix that file.

When the platform itself will not let you resize images or remove a mandatory slider, the limitation is real. Describe what you cannot turn off and what the guest needs to see first. A faster page is often a smaller page. That is a content decision as much as a technical one.

Frequently asked questions

Should we lazy-load every image?

Load the first screen normally so the welcome is immediate. Delay images that sit far down the page, not the picture that explains where you are.

Is a slow giving page a problem?

Yes. People who already decided to give should not fight a spinner. Test that path on a phone the same way you test the homepage.
