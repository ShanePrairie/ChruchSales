Help People Find Small Groups on Your Church Website

A groups page should help someone ask to join without publishing a private address or a vague list of names.

November 25, 2026By Modernized Ministry4 min read

A groups page should help someone ask to join without publishing a private address or a vague list of names.

Publish the decision facts, not the roster

Someone looking for a group needs the day, the general time, whether it is open to newcomers, who it is for, and how to ask. They do not need a public list of members, a private home address, or a photo of someone's living room with the street visible. Offer a request path that lets a leader share the address after a real conversation.

Label the page with words guests use, such as small groups or community groups, and put it in the menu. The navigation guide is the reminder not to hide this under a brand name that only insiders know. If groups pause for a season, say the next start date instead of leaving a dead directory.

  • Day and general time, such as weekday evening.
  • Who the group is for, including age or life stage if that is real.
  • Whether childcare is available.
  • Whether new people can join now.
  • How to ask, and who replies.

Describe the purpose in one or two sentences

Say what the group actually does: studies a book of the Bible, shares a meal, prays, or practices a language. Avoid a paragraph that could describe any gathering in any church. If a group is full, mark it full. If it is only for members, say so before someone requests a seat.

Link groups that exist to support a conviction back to the beliefs page when that context matters, without turning every listing into a doctrinal essay. A sentence and a link are enough. People can decide if the conversation is one they want.

Make the ask a conversation, not a data grab

A short form can collect a name, a way to reply, and which group they mean. Explain that a leader will follow up and that joining is a conversation, not an automatic enrollment. Do not require a long testimony. Do not add people to a text list because they asked about a group. The visitor follow-up plan is a separate permission from a group inquiry.

Reply in the time you promised. If leaders are volunteers, set a realistic window and name a backup person at the church office when a leader is away. A form that lands in an unchecked inbox teaches people that the website is decorative.

Protect homes and personal patterns

Do not publish a street address for a group that meets in a home. A neighborhood name is enough until a leader confirms the person is coming. Do not list children's names. Do not post a leader's personal cell number if they have not agreed to receive cold calls from the open web. An office email or a form is safer.

If a group meets at the church building, you can name the campus and the night. Still avoid publishing a minute-by-minute list of who is in which room if that creates a safety concern. Public information should help a person take a step, not map everyone's week.

Review the directory when the season turns

Groups change in January and in the fall, and sometimes midstream when a leader moves. Put a review on the calendar. Remove groups that ended. Update childcare notes. A stale directory is worse than a short one, because people make plans around it.

Tell leaders what they are allowed to change themselves. A leader can usually update a time or mark a group full. They should not rewrite the church's beliefs in a group blurb or add a private address. A one-page note for leaders prevents the directory from drifting. When a new group starts, require the same fields as the others so the page stays scannable.

If the list is too messy to maintain by hand, simplify it to a few open groups and a sentence that says other groups exist and how to ask. Describe how groups start and end if you want the page structured so a volunteer can update a listing without breaking the layout. The goal is a true invitation, not a complete census.

Frequently asked questions

Should sermon-based groups be listed separately?

Only if the rhythm is different. Otherwise list them with the others and say they discuss the Sunday passage. A second directory is another page to forget.

What if we have only two groups?

Publish two accurate listings. A short honest page is more useful than a tool that implies a catalog you do not have.
