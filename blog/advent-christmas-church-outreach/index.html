Advent and Christmas Outreach: A Church Website and Welcome Plan

Christmas outreach works when the website states the extra services clearly and the welcome stays human after the holiday.

November 25, 2026By Modernized Ministry4 min read

Christmas outreach works when the website states the extra services clearly and the welcome stays human after the holiday.

Publish the special schedule before you decorate the homepage

Guests at Christmas are often deciding among several services, sometimes with relatives in town. Put the date, time, and place of each service in text on the homepage and on the events page as soon as the church has decided. Say whether a service is especially aimed at children, whether it includes communion, and about how long it lasts. A snowfall photo does not carry that information.

Take the seasonal block down when the services are over, and restore the ordinary Sunday time the same week. The Easter plan is a different season with a similar discipline: dates first, mood second. Do not leave Christmas copy up through Lent.

  • Each special service: date, time, place, and length.
  • What is different from a normal Sunday.
  • Parking or overflow plans if you expect a larger crowd.
  • Whether childcare or a children's moment is offered.
  • How to ask an accessibility question before the night.

Write invitations that respect a rare visit

Many people will attend once and not return in January. The website can still be kind. Explain what will happen, what to wear in the same voice you use the rest of the year, and that guests do not need to know the songs. Skip countdown language that shames anyone who is unsure. A clear invitation is enough.

If you offer an Advent series, a concert, or a meal, give each its own listing on the events page with an end time. Do not merge three nights into one vague banner. People plan rides and childcare around end times.

Prepare the building details that change under a crowd

Larger services break ordinary instructions. If you open a side lot, add chairs, or change the children's plan, write the exception where the usual instruction lives, and date it. Tell people which door to use if the main door will be crowded. Note accessible seating if the extra chairs change the aisles.

If you print invitations or send a short email, use the same times as the website. Pick one person to compare the paper, the email, and the homepage the week you publish. A relative who was handed a card with last year's time should still be rescued by the website, which means the website has to be the version you trust. Say on the card that times are confirmed on the site.

If a service needs a headcount for a meal, say what happens when you reach capacity and whether a waiting family can still attend worship. Do not leave that answer only in a social reply.

Update the map listing and the plan-your-visit page so they do not contradict the homepage. A guest who follows the wrong time because three pages disagree will not experience the welcome you designed. Assign one person to check all three the week of the services.

Plan the week after, quietly

Decide before Christmas Eve what you will say to guests who left a name. A short note that thanks them, states the next ordinary Sunday time, and offers a way to ask a question is enough. Do not enroll them in a long campaign because they came to a concert. The seven-day welcome can be adapted, with permission, and then stopped if they do not respond.

If you collect nothing, you can still put the January Sunday time on the homepage the next morning. The people who meant to come back will look. Make the path obvious. Do not make them hunt under a banner that still says Christmas Eve.

Retire the seasonal site on purpose

Put a takedown date on the office calendar when you publish the schedule. Remove special forms that still accept registrations. Archive photos that were only for those nights if permission was limited to the event. Return navigation labels to their ordinary words.

If you want help lining up the homepage, the events page, and a follow-up that does not outrun consent, describe the services you host. Seasonal clarity is a content project with a deadline, which is why it belongs on a calendar rather than in a last-minute slide.

Frequently asked questions

Should Christmas Eve replace the normal Sunday time on the homepage?

Show both while both are true. Label the special service as special, and keep the regular Sunday visible so members and guests are not guessing.

How is this different from an Easter plan?

The mechanics are similar, but the gatherings, family patterns, and music are not. Write the details for the services you are actually holding this December.
